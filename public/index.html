What today proved
The "ground truth" thesis is real — and expensive. No reconciled record means six-figure disputes and broken trust. That is the product, not a feature of it.
The incumbent stack is a dinosaur. QuickBooks at best, spreadsheets, or the contractor's wife doing the books. Greenfield.
We already built what he described. Vendor master, approvals, change orders, ledger, audit trail. We're aiming the engine, not starting it.
What today complicated
Adoption is the risk — but the owner is the lever. ~7% of contractors use software. Ryan's fix: train the client, and the client makes the contractor modernize.
Don't hold the money. The owner approves; the system pays from the owner's own bank. Ryan refuses to touch funds — the trust-account liability is brutal.
A funded rival in the room. Ryan is $250K into an owner-facing AI agent, with Rippling's Matt Plank circling, 730 clients, and a $150/mo model.
The marketplace is premature. It erodes the trusted-vendor moat and licensing bounds it. Park it; don't pitch it.
Still open: who we build for first — the GC, or the owner.
